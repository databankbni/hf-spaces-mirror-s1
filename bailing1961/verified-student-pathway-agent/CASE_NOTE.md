# One-page case note

## What problem does it solve?

Course and admission information is often spread across many pages. Prospective students can struggle to work out which information applies to them, whether they appear to meet published conditions and what to do next. Ordinary chatbots may answer fluently while mixing course years, overlooking conditions or sounding more certain than the evidence allows.

## Who is it for?

Prospective students, career changers, education advisers and education providers exploring a safer online enquiry service.

## What does a visitor provide?

A visitor can ask a course question or provide a small set of non-identifying details: applicant type, current qualification, relevant field, work experience, English evidence and intended course.

## What does it return?

- A concise course answer with a source and effective date.
- A preliminary comparison with published entry conditions.
- Missing evidence or conditions that cannot be confirmed.
- A possible study pathway supported by the demo knowledge set.
- A compact 4C verification summary and human-review boundary.

## What are the safety boundaries?

The demo does not make admission decisions, guarantee credit, give visa or immigration advice, promise employment outcomes or store personal information. Unclear qualifications, exceptions and credit decisions are referred for human review. The included catalogue is fictional and must not be used for a real application.

## What remains protected?

The public Space shows the customer experience, sample rules and selected verification outputs. The production platform for document parsing, LLM Wiki construction, provenance, rule extraction, constraint checking, audit, evaluation, human approval and deployment is separate proprietary capability.
