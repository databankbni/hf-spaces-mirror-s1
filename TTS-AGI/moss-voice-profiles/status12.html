Status 12 — notation, ranking, and a block that was never generated

Round 3 of the voice-profile corpus. Six complaints from the listening pass; five correct as stated, one a symptom of something worse.

The finding that matters most: the page was serving audio from a different run than the scores it displayed. All 89 regenerated groups played their pre-regeneration take underneath a “NEW, improved” badge. One group whose metadata said 5.76 s shipped a 0.24 s clip.

So the report that “intoxication is still too low” and “the disfluency levels are not clearly different” was a report about the old audio. The regeneration had never actually been heard.

1. The publishing bug

The exporter matched each group's winning take by audio_key, which is only <gid>.cNNN.mp3 — not unique across runs. rewritten/, rewritten48/ and regen/ all mint the same names and two of them share the profile tag PRW, so whichever tar was scanned first won.

Fixed by matching on (source_dir, audio_key), clearing the output directory before writing, and asserting each exported file's byte size is consistent with its metadata duration.

Second trap: rewritten/ is 16 kHz and rewritten48/ is the same takes at 48 kHz. Picking the “newest” directory silently downgrades the published audio. Choose by sample rate, not mtime.

2. Notation — and how it corrupted the scoring

Round brackets carry delivery cues and vocal bursts; square brackets carry pauses; tags are lowercase because a capitalised token is spelled out letter by letter.

GENERAL: <standing description of voice and situation>
SCRIPT:
(delivery cue) "the line, (sobs) with tags inline, [pause] and pauses in squares"

Our pipeline emitted <sobs>. The WER reference stripped \([^)]*\) — round brackets only — so an angle-bracket tag stayed in the reference string and every burst-tagged take was charged word error for failing to pronounce “sobs”. Reward is multiplied by (1 − WER): the ranker was selecting against exactly the takes the tags were added to help.

This invalidates the round-2 conclusion that inline tags win on only 6 of 12 edge cases. That ablation ran with the broken stripper. It has been re-run — see §5.

Verified in generation: [pause] is not spoken. ASR of takes containing it returns clean text with no “pause” token.

3. Ranking — re-derived on 28,698 stored candidates

Two failure modes, in opposite directions. Short takes had a perverse advantage (fewer words → fewer chances to misread → WER near zero over a nearly silent clip). And nothing stopped a runaway: one 28 s rendering of a 20-word line, whose ASR wanders into “meant in staying in the dist…”, outranked an 8 s take of the same line at a quarter of the word error, because rambling accumulates emotional content.

  • Raw beats z-scored, and that is where the whole effect is. z-scoring normalises blend within a group, so blend stops competing across groups. Going raw moves median winner blend 3.65 → 4.67 (+28 %). Swapping the WER form alone moved only 28 of 808 winners.
  • We do not adopt the manual's /(1+WER). The per-emotion pages specify (genu + blend + 1.25·emo) / (1+WER). Measured, the inverse form is too lenient to hold a runaway down and made runaways more common (18 vs 14). The harsher ×(1−WER) is kept — a deliberate deviation, with the numbers for it.
  • The length-fit ceiling is the real fix. Expected duration = words / 2.8; free up to 1.6×, quadratic falloff beyond. Runaways 14 → 7 with the blend gain kept.
  • The cost is genuineness: 0.738 → 0.626 (−15 %). A real trade, stated rather than buried.
  • The duration floor alone changed zero of 808 winners under the old score. It is a safety net, not a fix — it matters only once raw scoring is in play (sub-3 s winners 9 → 0).

Character groups had the emotion term switched off: their target is a voice, not a feeling, and falling through to emo_strength would represent the dragon by its angriest reading rather than its most draconic one.

4. The missing character block — 808 ≠ 832

The plan advertises 832 groups per voice; the matrix built 808. The missing 24 are 12 character LoRAs × {EN, DE}, absent for a reason that leaves no error message: the repo had been fetched with allow_patterns that pulled every adapter_config.json but only one adapter_model.safetensors. A config-only directory looks resolvable to any check that stops at the config, so the block passed pre-flight and then vanished.

6. What was applied — and what was reverted

118 matrix groups were regenerated, but adoption is decided on group means over all 32 candidates, per (recipe × language) cell, not per group. Means because the ranking formula changed between rounds, so a top-1 comparison would measure the recipe and the ranker at once. Per cell because picking the better of two runs group by group selects on the same noise being measured, which manufactures an improvement out of nothing. A cell that did not improve keeps the original take — a recipe that lost is reverted, not shipped with a badge claiming it won.

13 of 18 cells adopted. The headline reversals:

  • The moderate broken-speech treatment (L2) is a negative result in English — −0.224 over 32 groups, and round 3's ellipsis-only version was flat against base too. Two rounds have now failed to make it work; those 32 groups keep their original take. It does work in German (+0.113), which is why the decision is per language.
  • Edge-case bursts help in English (+0.11 to +0.24) and hurt in German (−0.08 to −0.41). Adopted for EN, reverted for DE.
  • Everything heavy won in both languages: disfluency +0.72, the L3 emotions (intoxication, grief, hesitancy) +0.58 / +0.68, and the explicitness LoRA +5.5 blend.
