Why it happens — two different causes

1. The genre miss is an out-of-vocabulary problem. LeVo's descriptions field feeds the type_info conditioner, and the model ships its actual tag vocabulary in sample/description/: 2 genders, 27 genres, 8 emotions, 7 timbres. opera is not in it. Neither is choir, gospel, hymn or a cappella. Out-of-vocabulary words are not rejected — they are conditioned on weakly, and the model falls back to its prior, which is pop.

2. The backing choir is a chorus-section phenomenon. The benchmark already showed this could not be fixed downstream: LeVo scored B = 4.07 on the mix and 4.07 again after stem separation, while every other model's B rose once instruments were stripped. Nothing instrumental was there to remove — the non-compliance is a second voice. The search localised it: of the clips scoring S ≤ 3, the judge names the chorus unprompted in 50 % of cases.

"A female voice sings the verse alone, but multi-part vocal harmonies join in during the chorus"
"I hear a male singer start solo, but at the chorus (0:15) a full stack of vocal harmonies joins in behind him"
"The clip begins with a solo female voice, but at 0:08 a choir/backing vocal ensemble enters"

[chorus] is a learned cue for stacked, doubled, harmonised vocals, because that is what choruses are in the training corpus. Delete the chorus and the choir goes with it.

The recipe

descriptions = "<gender>, <IN-VOCABULARY genre tag>, <free style words>"
lyrics       = "[verse] Line one. Line two. Line three. Line four. Line five. Line six."
gen_type     = "vocal"
temperature  = 0.9    top_k = 50    top_p = 0.0    cfg_coef = 2.0
  1. Anchor the genre on one of LeVo's 27 genre tags, then add your real style words after it. classical carries opera; opera alone does not.
  2. One [verse] block. No [chorus]. If you need two sections, tag the second one [verse] as well.
  3. Never write [intro-*], [inst-*] or [outro-*]. They are instrumental by definition and they put instruments back into a vocals-only render.
  4. Do not write negations and do not write a cappella. Both cost score. The solo is bought by structure and by gen_type='vocal', not by asking.
  5. CFG 2.0. It is the genre-fidelity knob; 1.0 is as bad as an empty prompt.

One honest wrinkle. The choir winner the search converged on still carries a negation stack, which contradicts the advice above. It got there because the negations are inert for choir, not because they helped — the controlled probe below tested exactly that and found no effect in either direction. The load-bearing parts of that prompt are the classical anchor, the sacred style word, the single [verse] and CFG 2.0. Note also that choir itself must never appear: what is wanted is choral style sung by one person, and the baseline's gospel, choir is why it was the worst of the four.

What did not work

  • Naming the genre in plain English. female, opera was the worst opera prompt in the search (S 3.00 / G 3.17) — worse than saying nothing about the genre beyond classical.
  • a cappella — the single most destructive phrase found. It takes opera's genre score from 4.17 to 1.83. Almost certainly because "a cappella" labels a cappella groups in a music corpus: close-harmony vocal bands, the opposite of a solo aria. unaccompanied is the safe alternative and was the best opera cell in that probe.
  • Anything at all in the instrument slot, when gen_type='vocal'. Proved by manipulation check: adding piano did not add a piano — I stayed at 5.00, because the bgm codebook is overwritten before rendering. The slot cannot add instruments, so it cannot remove them either; it only perturbs the voice.
  • Seven extra generations of evolution. Generations 4–10 produced no improvement over generation 3. The three designed sweeps found everything there was to find; the mutation phase only drifted inside the noise band. Their value was statistical — they are why the winners have n = 12–30 and the baselines n = 60. Budget three designed generations and spend the rest on replication.
  • The recipe on world-music genres. It made genre match worse (family mean G 1.79 → 1.53). Anchoring joik, khayal, Tuvan throat singing or Bulgarian village singing on folk drags them toward generic Western folk. There is no in-vocabulary tag for any of them — a model capability limit, not a prompting problem.
  • The recipe on urban genres (reggae, dancehall, lovers rock, afrobeats) — clean-pass regressed 62 % → 38 %. The single long [verse] that buys solo compliance elsewhere appears to cost rhythmically-driven genres their structure.
  • Fixing it downstream. A vocal separator cannot remove a second voice from a voice; that is why the benchmark's B score did not move after stem separation.

Method

  • Search. 10 generations × 7 candidates + a constant baseline anchor riding in every batch, 6 replicate seeds per candidate per generation, statistics accumulated across generations. Generations 1–3 were designed sweeps (description content, lyric structure, sampling); 4 onward were elitist mutation and crossover.
  • Judge. Gemini 3.6 Flash, three axes, every level anchored in words, audio delivered at 160 kbps stereo (grading through a 48 kbps codec grades the codec). Every judgement cached. 2523 clips judged.
  • Generation harness. Batched LeVo with a static KV cache and a CUDA-graphed decode step. Fixed batch size 8 for the entire search — LeVo's outputs are not reproducible across batch sizes. Three batch>1 correctness bugs are patched out: the batch-wide EOS truncation, the row-0-only repetition penalty, and Tango.code2sound's decode_audio(...)[0], which silently returns one song for a whole batch (the vocoder therefore runs at batch 1 here).
  • Per-row RNG. Codebook 0 is sampled from a per-row torch.Generator, so a row's audio is a function of (prompt, sampling params, seed) alone and not of its batch-mates or its position in the batch. Verified bit-identical for the same genome at two positions. Without this, two candidates in the same batch are not comparable.
  • Realness. Every clip is checked for duration, RMS and fraction of frames above −40 dBFS before it is judged.
