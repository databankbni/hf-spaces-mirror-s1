⚠️ Important Notes Before You Start

  • Telegraph Hill Blvd is the main road near Coit Tower - this is your start/end point
  • Many "streets" are actually footpaths, steps, or boardwalks - wear comfortable walking shoes
  • The Filbert Steps and Greenwich Steps are the most famous pathways with gardens
  • Union Street is the only feeder street for vehicle access to the upper hill
  • Some paths are residential access only - please respect private property
  • The parrots of Telegraph Hill may be spotted in the gardens!
  • Bring water - there are steep climbs and many stairs

🚶 Turn-by-Turn Route

  1. 1
    Paved Start at 117 Telegraph Hill Boulevard South
    Begin at the property near Coit Tower. Head south on Telegraph Hill Blvd toward Pioneer Park and the Filbert Steps access.
    This is near the highest point of Telegraph Hill (284 ft elevation)
  2. 2
    Steps Descend the Filbert Street Steps East ↓
    Walk down the famous Filbert Steps (about 400 wooden steps) through the Grace Marchant Gardens. This is a footpath/stairway descending the east slope.
    Pass the Malloch Building (1360 Montgomery) - featured in "Dark Passage" film. Watch for parrots in the gardens!
  3. 3
    Paved Filbert Street (lower section) to Montgomery West
    At the bottom of the steps, reach the lower Filbert Street stub. Walk west to Montgomery Street.
    The intersection of Montgomery & Filbert Steps is just outside the historic district boundary
  4. 4
    Paved Montgomery Street - North to Union North ↑
    Head north on Montgomery Street. Pass the historic buildings at 1301-1315 Montgomery (ca. 1850-1865). Continue to Union Street intersection.
    1301 Montgomery is possibly SF's oldest brick building
  5. 5
    Paved Union Street - West to Calhoun split West ←
    Turn left (west) on Union Street. Walk past historic homes including 287-291 Union (Cooney House, ca. 1854-1857). Continue to where Union splits for Calhoun Terrace.
    Union is the only vehicular feeder to the upper hill
  6. 6
    Alley Calhoun Terrace - Full length South ↓
    Take the Calhoun Terrace branch. Walk the full length of this narrow dead-end alley (1-70 range). Pass the Kahn House at 66-70 Calhoun (Richard Neutra, 1939).
    Calhoun is almost flat at upper terrace, then drops steeply. Retrace to Union.
  7. 7
    Paved Union Street - Continue West & return West ←
    Return to Union and continue west to the end of the accessible portion. Then turn back east on Union.
    Union drops 170 ft in 185 ft to Sansome - very steep section ahead
  8. 8
    Cliff Union Street Steps to Sansome (if accessible) East ↓
    Carefully descend the steep Union Street steps/footpath toward Sansome Street. The foot of cliffs is reinforced/fenced.
    ⚠️ Very steep cliff section - 170 ft drop. Some sections may be impassable.
  9. 9
    Paved Sansome Street - North to Greenwich North ↑
    At Sansome Street (eastern boundary), head north to the Greenwich Street steps access.
    Sansome is the eastern boundary of the historic district
  10. 10
    Steps Greenwich Street Steps - Ascend West ↑
    Walk up the Greenwich Steps (footpath with gardens, similar to Filbert). Ascend to Montgomery Street.
    Valetta's Gardens line this path. The cliff leaps 40 ft in 40 ft here.
  11. 11
    Paved Montgomery Street - North to Alta North ↑
    Head north on Montgomery to upper Alta Street intersection (near 1254-1262 Montgomery, the district's highest point at 218 ft).
    Julius' Castle was at the northern dead-end of Montgomery
  12. 12
    Alley Alta Street - Upper section & down South ↓
    Take upper Alta Street (almost flat section near Montgomery). Walk the full length, then continue down the steep lower section toward Sansome.
    Upper Alta is ~202 ft elevation, drops 150 ft in 185 ft to Sansome. Retrace if needed.
  13. 13
    Boardwalk Napier Lane - Full length East →
    From near the Filbert Steps/Montgomery area, access Napier Lane. Walk this charming narrow boardwalked street (8-34 range, homes from 1875-1890).
    One of the most charming 1870s neighborhoods in SF. Grace Marchant lived at the Filbert & Napier corner.
  14. 14
    Boardwalk Darrell Place (formerly Norton Place) - Full length East →
    Walk the full length of Darrell Place boardwalk footpath (20-62 range).
    Planking path. Built by longshoremen in the 1850s-1870s.
  15. 15
    Steps Green Street - Explore accessible sections South ↓
    Navigate to Green Street (220-260 range). Explore the accessible upper sections. Green drops 140 ft in about 150 linear ft - extremely steep!
    ⚠️ Some sections may be impassable. Modern Sanborn maps still read "Impassable for Teams."
  16. 16
    Paved Telegraph Hill Boulevard - Return to start North ↑
    From the upper Montgomery/Union area, make your way back north to Telegraph Hill Boulevard and return to 117.
    End of route - you've now covered all major streets, alleys, and pathways!

💡 Pro Tips for Minimal Backtracking

  • This route is designed as a modified Eulerian path - covering edges with minimal repetition
  • Dead-end alleys (Alta, Calhoun) require out-and-back travel - that's unavoidable
  • The Filbert Steps descent + Greenwich Steps ascent creates an efficient loop
  • Union Street is used as a spine to access Calhoun and connect areas
  • Some private pathways exist but are not publicly accessible - respect residents' privacy

🏛️ Historic Highlights You'll See

  • Coit Tower - Art Deco tower at the summit (near start)
  • Malloch Building (1360 Montgomery) - Streamline Moderne, "Dark Passage" film location
  • Cooney House (291 Union) - Built ca. 1854, possibly oldest documented building on the Hill
  • Andrews House (31 Alta) - Built 1852, one of SF's oldest extant houses
  • "Duck House" (60-62 Alta) - William Wurster, 1936; Armistead Maupin lived here
  • Kahn House (66-70 Calhoun) - Richard Neutra, International Style, 1939
  • Grace Marchant Gardens - Filbert Steps gardens, started in 1949
  • Julius' Castle (now closed) - Historic castle-shaped restaurant at Montgomery dead-end
