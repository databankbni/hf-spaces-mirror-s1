Your model already knows more than it tells you.

A language model does not give you an answer. It gives you a probability distribution over answers, and generating text draws one sample from it. Ask the same question twice and you can get two different results. Most people treat that as a defect. It is an untapped resource.

Why counting votes works this well

Correct answers agree with each other. Wrong answers scatter.

A wrong reasoning chain has a thousand available ways to be wrong, and picks a different one each time, so errors fragment into singletons. The correct answer is the only thing several attempts can converge on together. Over N samples the right answer accumulates mass while the wrong ones divide it — which is why the mode can be correct even when correct trajectories are a minority of the pool.

The model does not need to be right most of the time. It needs to be right more consistently than it is wrong in any one particular way.

Where the gain actually comes from

Most reports skip this, and it decides whether a headline means anything. Comparing one sample against an N-sample system quietly counts two different things as method:

a single sample45.3%
random among the trajectories that answered 46.3%+1.0
majority vote 66.5%+20.2
total+21.2

With more trajectories, one of them usually did not abstain — and that alone lifts a random pick, without any selection happening. Separating it shows that 20.2 of the 21.2 points, 95% of the gain, is genuine selection. We publish the split because the size of that first term is not knowable in advance: at a tighter token budget the same experiment gave it five times as much of the credit.

Against random selection at the same N, exact McNemar clears p ≤ 6.6 × 10⁻⁵ at every one of 200 random seeds, with a median of 8.2 × 10⁻¹⁰. That is the worst of the 200, not the best — a single seed would have let us print a far prettier number.

You do not have to believe any of this

pip install "bestofn[math]"
python scripts/analyse.py

All 25,600 trajectories are published in full — the complete reasoning text of every sample, with token counts, finish reasons and log-probabilities. Not a summary. The analysis script re-extracts every answer from that raw text rather than re-counting stored ones, so it catches a parsing bug instead of inheriting one. Every number on this page comes back out in about two minutes, on a laptop, with no GPU.

A note on what this page used to say. An earlier version reported results on AIME 2024 and credited a trained verifier with a 16.6-point gain over majority voting. Those measurements were produced by version 1.0.0 of this software, which contained defects in answer extraction and in the handling of truncated trajectories, and they are not reproducible with the corrected implementation. The verifier was never published, and in the one evidence file that was, it selected the same answer as majority voting on every problem. Those claims are withdrawn and should not be cited. Everything on this page is GSM8K, measured with bestofn 1.1.5, and reproducible from the published dataset.
