The short version

This app listens to you read Arabic aloud so it can tell you how fluently you read it. Your recordings are not kept. Each clip is analysed and deleted within seconds. Nothing about your reading leaves your device except the audio needed to score it, and no account is required.

There is one exception that matters, and it is set out in full below: on some browsers, a fallback feature sends audio to Google or Apple.

What we process, and why

Voice recordings

What. Short audio clips of you reading a displayed line, or saying a single displayed word.

Why. To measure how you read — where you paused, whether a word came out whole or in pieces. This is the entire purpose of the app.

Where it goes. The clip is sent to our analysis service, converted to a plain audio format, aligned against the text you were shown, and then deleted. The file exists on the server for the length of one request, typically a few seconds. It is never written to a database, a storage bucket, or a log.

How long it is kept. It is not kept.

What we get back and store. Only the result — for example "word 3 was read as one unit". That result is stored in your browser, not on our servers.

Your progress

Which words you have mastered, your streak, and your session history live in your browser's local storage on your device. They are never transmitted to us. Clear your browser data, or use the "Reset progress" button, and they are gone permanently — including from our reach, because we never had them.

Server logs

Our hosting providers keep ordinary web server logs — IP address, timestamp, browser type — as every website does. We do not use these to build a profile of you, and we do not connect them to your recordings.

The exception: browser speech recognition

Where our analysis service is unavailable, the app can fall back to your browser's built-in speech recognition to check which word you said.

On Chrome and Edge, this sends your audio to Google. On Safari, to Apple. It is the same mechanism as the dictation button on your keyboard, and it is governed by Google's and Apple's privacy policies, not ours. We never see that audio, and we cannot delete it on your behalf.

You can avoid this entirely by using the app while our analysis service is connected — the header tells you which mode you are in. Refusing microphone access disables both paths.

A note on voice and the law

Voice recordings are personal data under UK and EU data protection law, so we treat them as such even though we discard them immediately.

They are not being used as biometric data. We are not identifying you from your voice, comparing it to anyone else's, or building a voiceprint. The model measures timing and sound against a text you were already shown. That distinction matters legally, and we are not relying on it to cut corners — we simply do not keep the audio at all.

Our lawful basis is your consent, given when you allow microphone access. You

can withdraw it at any time by denying microphone permission or closing the app. Because we retain nothing, withdrawal is immediate and complete.

Your rights

You have the right to access, correct, delete, or export your personal data, and to complain to a supervisory authority.

In practice these are easy to satisfy here: we hold no recordings and no account, and your progress data is on your own device where you can delete it yourself. If you believe we hold something about you, ask and we will tell you honestly.

Children

This app is not designed for children and we do not knowingly collect data from anyone under 13. If you are a parent and believe your child has used it, contact us — though in practice we will hold nothing to delete.

If this changes

Two planned features would change the picture materially, and this policy will be rewritten before either ships, not after:

  • Storing recordings to check the app's judgements against a qualified teacher's ear. That would turn "not kept" into "kept, for a stated period, with your explicit and separate consent" — which is a different promise and will be asked for separately.
  • Accounts, so progress follows you between devices. That would mean storing an email address and your progress on our servers.

Neither is live today.
