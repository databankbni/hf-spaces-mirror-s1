وَصْل · Wasl Prototype

Two drills. About three minutes.

First, words flashed too fast to sound out — you say each one aloud. Then three sentences read aloud while the app listens for where you pause.

What it's actually measuring

The flash drill shows a word for a fraction of a second, then asks you to say it. Two things make that a real test: the exposure is too short to sound anything out, and you cannot pronounce a shape you did not read — so remembering the outline gets you nowhere. It also times how long you take to start speaking.

The reading drill does two things at once: it checks which words it heard, and it listens for silences inside them. Assembling ك‑ت‑ا‑ب before saying it leaves gaps that fluent reading doesn't.

Speech recognition on non-native Arabic is unreliable — error rates run far higher than for native speakers. So it is only ever used to confirm a word, never to fail one. If it can't make you out, you get a dotted line and a slower path to mastery, not a red mark.

The gold marks are scaffolding. Master a word and its vowel marks dim, then vanish.
