What CRIA is for the river

One validated view of the whole basin

The Colorado River supplies roughly 40 million people and is over-allocated and drying. Its science lives in many excellent but separate tools. CRIA is the layer that ties them together: it reads the basin from snowfall to shortage, validates every signal against NASA observations, and presents it in the units managers plan in — acre-feet and Lake Mead shortage tiers.

Who it's for

Built for the people who manage the river

CRIA is a curated, observation-validated visualization tool from the Center for Hydrologic Innovations (CHI) at Arizona State University. It is built first for the people who must turn basin hydrology into decisions — and the researchers and analysts who support them.

Primary user
Technical water manager

Operations & planning staff at agencies such as the Central Arizona Project, the U.S. Bureau of Reclamation and state water authorities — who translate basin hydrology into allocation, shortage-tier and long-term planning decisions.

Also serves
Hydrology & climate researchers

Scientists at CHI and partner institutions who need one validated, integrated basin picture — surface water, snowpack and subsurface storage together — instead of a dozen separate datasets and units.

Also serves
Water-policy analysts

Staff who need the science framed in the terms decisions are made in — acre-feet, Lake Mead shortage tiers and the Law of the River — with every figure traceable to its source.

CRIA's contribution

What CRIA brings together

Beyond reproducing published findings, CRIA provides integrated decision-support diagnostics built from a validated multi-source record — brought together in a single workflow, rather than scattered across multiple tools and studies.

Integration
One validated basin view
Surface water, snowpack and subsurface storage in a single sub-basin picture, evaluated against NASA GRACE and SMAP.
Attribution
GRACE storage → groundwater
A basin-scale groundwater-loss diagnosis inferred from NASA GRACE total water-storage anomalies and cross-checked against the modelled water balance.
Sensitivity
Interactive ΔT / ΔP engine
Warming and precipitation dialled to projected runoff, with 95% confidence intervals, expressed in acre-feet.
Diagnostics
Tool-built risk indices
Composite indicators — the Aridity Severity Index and a Compound Water-System Risk index — constructed from the validated record.
Validation
Evidence shown in-app
Multi-sensor validation is displayed in the tool itself, each headline result placed beside the peer-reviewed study that corroborates it independently.
Transparency
Established vs. open, stated
Confirmed results are separated from open questions, so the state of knowledge is presented plainly and never overstated.

Statistical methods & uncertainty

Leave-One-Out (LOO) cross-validation
Each water year is predicted from a fit on all the others — honest out-of-sample skill, not in-sample fit.
95% prediction & bootstrap confidence intervals
Every forecast carries a stated uncertainty band; sensitivities are resampled 2,000× to bound them.
Mann–Kendall trend tests
Non-parametric trend detection with 95% confidence — robust to non-normal hydrologic data.
Hydrologic elasticity
Streamflow response to warming and precipitation from ln(Q) = a + b·ln(P) + c·T, fitted on the record.
GRACE − VIC residual
Groundwater attribution: the storage decline not explained by modelled snow and soil is isolated and tested.
Why it's different

The gap CRIA fills

From scattered studies to one decision-ready tool

Snowpack, storage, drought and projections live in separate tools and different units, and surface and subsurface water are rarely shown together. CRIA closes that gap — a single, sub-basin, observation-validated view of the whole Colorado River Basin, with uncertainty stated and every figure traceable to its source. That integration, at this resolution, with this validation, is rarely available in a single tool.

Concretely, CRIA brings together the fall soil-moisture control on water-year runoff (leave-one-out R² = 0.45; Ghimire, Vivoni & Wang 2026) beside a model-independent GRACE basin storage decline (p = 10⁻¹⁹; Abdelmohsen et al. 2025), on top of a reanalysis validated at streamflow NSE ≈ 0.96 and against NASA SMAP soil moisture (R² 0.71–0.81; Wang et al. 2026) — surface and subsurface, one sub-basin view, in acre-feet. Each of these findings was published separately; CRIA is where a manager sees them together, in the same units and on the same map.
